## `all_400_images_for_labeling.zip` — findings

- **400 images total, 100 per class** (`red`, `yellow`, `green`, `sample`), perfectly balanced by filename prefix.
- **Zero label files.** No `.txt` YOLO annotations, no `data.yaml`, nothing. This archive is exactly what its name says: raw material *for* labeling, not a labeled dataset.
- All sampled images are already **224×224**, single object, **on a plain white/gray background**, no other class and no clutter in frame.
- This is **not** the dataset that trained `best.pt`. The checkpoint's `train_args.data` points at `/content/custom_data/custom_data/data.yaml` on a Google Colab instance — that labeled dataset (with whatever box annotations were actually used) was never included in what I received, so **I cannot verify box quality, train/val split, or annotation correctness** for the data that produced `best.pt`. I have not invented labels to fill this gap.

## `Seperate Images.zip` — findings

- Structure: `Model/{red_,Yellow_,Greeen_,Sample_}/` — each folder has ~12 raw photos of *only that one class* plus a small (~967 KB) `.tflite` file.
- I parsed one of those tflite files (`Red_patient.tflite`): input `[1,192,192,3]` INT8, output `[1,2268,6]` INT8 (already-decoded `x,y,w,h,score,class`). This is Seeed's own **"Swift-YOLO" single-class model-zoo format** (192×192, end-to-end output) — a completely different architecture and export convention from your custom YOLO26n `best.pt` (224×224, raw multi-scale head). These four files are single-class practice/calibration models from Seeed's guided per-object training flow; they are not part of, and should not be confused with, your 4-class competition model.

## Risk this creates for the competition scene

The reference photo you provided shows **four objects together on a patterned mat with a first-aid box in frame**. The only images I can actually inspect (`all_400_images_for_labeling.zip`) are the opposite of that: one isolated object per frame on a blank background. If the real training data behind `best.pt` looked similar (plausible, given it's the same photo-capture setup), the reported **mAP50 = 0.966** is measuring an easy distribution and may **not** transfer to multi-object, cluttered-background performance. This is a real, concrete risk — not a hypothetical one — but it is separate from the GV2/export issue and does not require retraining to investigate first: get the export/hardware path working, point the real camera at the real scene, and measure actual confidence/recall on the four simultaneous objects. If that testing shows the model missing objects or confusing classes against the real background, *that* is the trigger to collect and label proper multi-object scene photos — not a preemptive retrain.
